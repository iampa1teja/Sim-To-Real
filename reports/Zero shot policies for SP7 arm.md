# Retargeting, not raw checkpoints, unlocks SP7 zero-shot

No released open-weight policy can be expected to work zero-shot on the SP7 when used as published. Every primary source reviewed limits "zero-shot" to embodiments in the training mix, and independent tests of OpenVLA, MolmoAct, X-VLA and π0.5 on unseen arms report success collapsing to zero. Three learned candidates are realistic. **LAP-3B** is the only open checkpoint that claims more than 50% average zero-shot success on unseen arms. **GR00T N1.7** (base checkpoint, DROID relative-EEF tag) is already in the project's serving stack. **π0.5-DROID with FK→IK retargeting** reached 37–44% on a new arm or gripper in a controlled study, and its masked variant, Cloak, reached about 70%. Even those numbers come from authors' own labs, on Franka-like or YAM hardware rather than a hobby-servo arm. For cube-in-box and stacking, a non-learned modular pipeline (segmentation → GraspGen-X grasp → SP7 IK → rule- or VLM-chosen place pose) is the most dependable route that truly needs no demonstrations. The recommended path has three parts:

1. Build the modular pipeline as the zero-shot baseline.
2. Probe GR00T N1.7 and LAP-3B through an end-effector adapter in Isaac Lab, accepting low expected success.
3. Plan from the start on fine-tuning GR00T with scripted simulation demonstrations from the existing Phase 5 pipeline. Those are not human demonstrations and need no leader arm.

## Only end-effector-space checkpoints can drive SP7 at all

The first filter is the action space. The project's ZMQ clients emit absolute joint targets in the *policy's* joint space, and SP7's 7-joint kinematics match no training robot. Joint-space checkpoints therefore cannot command SP7 without a retargeting layer. That rules out direct use of all openpi DROID checkpoints. They output 7 joint values plus gripper, and π0.5-DROID was in fact trained on **joint velocity** actions, which openpi warns "are not compatible with simulated evaluation environments" ([openpi droid_policy.py](https://github.com/Physical-Intelligence/openpi/blob/main/src/openpi/policies/droid_policy.py), [README_train](https://github.com/Physical-Intelligence/openpi/blob/main/examples/droid/README_train.md)). The MolmoAct2 base checkpoint's normalization file also shows that its `franka_droid` tag is "absolute joint pose" in Franka radians, and its `so100_so101_molmoact2` tag is absolute joint pose in degree-like units for a 5-DoF arm ([MolmoAct2 norm_stats.json](https://huggingface.co/allenai/MolmoAct2/blob/main/norm_stats.json)). The MolmoAct2 the project currently serves is therefore a poor zero-shot fit for SP7 in either of its deployable heads.

The checkpoints whose outputs SP7's differential IK can execute directly are a short list.

| Candidate | Action output | Cameras / proprio | Licence | Documented zero-shot scope |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **LAP-3B** (`lihzha/LAP-3B`) | Delta EE pose, cm + Euler, +x forward/+y left/+z up | Up to 2 images at 224², Cartesian EE proprio | Apache-2.0 | Claims >50% average success on unseen 7-DoF Franka, YAM, Kinova ([LAP](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.10556v1)) |
| **GR00T N1.7-3B** base, DROID tag | Relative `eef_9d` (xyz + 6D rot) + absolute gripper, 40-step chunk | Exterior + wrist video, `eef_9d`/gripper/joint state | NVIDIA Open Model License; Cosmos-Reason2 backbone is gated | Pretrain tags only, "on datasets that match the pretrained embodiment" ([policy.md](https://github.com/NVIDIA/Isaac-GR00T/blob/main/getting_started/policy.md)) |
| MolmoAct2 base, `widowx_bridge` / `franka_molmoact` / `google_robot_*` tags | Delta EE pose, 3–10 step chunks | 1–4 cameras per tag | No HF licence tag | Base card calls it a fine-tuning foundation, "rather than a ready-to-run policy" ([card](https://huggingface.co/allenai/MolmoAct2)) |
| OpenVLA, Octo, SpatialVLA, CogACT, Magma, MolmoAct v1 | OXE 7-D delta EE + gripper | Mostly one third-person image | MIT / Apache-2.0 | Training-mix robots only ([OpenVLA](https://github.com/openvla/openvla), [SpatialVLA](https://huggingface.co/IPEC-COMMUNITY/spatialvla-4b-224-pt), [CogACT](https://huggingface.co/CogACT/CogACT-Base)) |
| RDT-1B | 128-D unified vector with EE position + 6D slots | Up to 3 views, proprio, control frequency | MIT | No unseen-robot result found ([RDT](https://huggingface.co/robotics-diffusion-transformer/rdt-1b)) |

GR00T N1.7's relative EEF space is the cleanest technical fit for Isaac Lab's relative-pose differential IK. NVIDIA calls the relative representation "a key factor in the model's cross-embodiment performance" ([Isaac-GR00T](https://github.com/NVIDIA/Isaac-GR00T)). It also matches SP7's sensor layout, since the DROID tag consumes exactly one exterior and one wrist view ([processor_config.json](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/GR00T-N1.7-3B/blob/main/processor_config.json)). Use only the `eef_9d` and gripper slices. The tag also emits a relative 7-joint delta that would fit SP7's vector length, but it carries Franka joint semantics. Two caveats remain. Posttrain tags passed to the base model "will fail with an error", so the base checkpoint with its pretrain tag is the one to probe. The exact `eef_9d` frame and 6D convention are not documented and must be read from the `gr00t_n1d7` code.

## Published numbers put realistic expectations at 0–45%

The evidence on genuinely new robots is thin but consistent. The LAP paper reports that the open baselines (π0.5-DROID, π0.5-Base, X-VLA, MolmoAct, OpenVLA) "all collapse to zero success" on its unseen arms, while LAP-3B averages above 50%, roughly twice the strongest baseline ([LAP](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.10556v1)). An independent group then measured LAP-VLA in Stanford's Cloak study. It scored **75% on the source robot but only 29–31% on a UMI gripper, a YAM arm and a Sharpa hand**. On the same targets, π0.5-DROID with tip-pose FK→IK retargeting scored **37.5–43.8% on the new gripper and arm**, and Cloak's wrist-view end-effector masking raised this to **68.8–70.8%** ([Cloak](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.22836v1)). LAP's self-reported figure and Cloak's measurement of it differ by about 20 points. A planning number of 30–50% for LAP on a well-calibrated Franka-like setup is therefore more defensible than the headline. SP7 sits further from that regime: it uses hobby servos, has a different reach, and has its own camera geometry.

The most cautionary data point comes from the closest analogue to SP7, a low-cost arm. A single-author community evaluation of the MolmoAct2 SO-100/101 checkpoint on community rigs outside its training data found action error about **twice that of simply holding the current pose**. The checkpoint produced "kinematically sane trajectories in the wrong part of the workspace" ([community blog](https://mcobzarenco-fontaine-blog.static.hf.space/posts/2026-08-10-molmoact2-oob-results.html)). This is anecdotal, and its extracted summary contains numbers that appear internally inconsistent. Its diagnosis still matches the controlled literature: the binding constraint is visual workspace calibration, not kinematics.

Even on the training robot, "zero-shot" is modest. Penn's 300-trial in-the-wild test of π0-FAST-DROID on a real DROID Franka measured **24% binary success on pick-and-place**, with 0% under unseen backgrounds ([Penn PAL Lab](https://penn-pal-lab.github.io/Pi0-Experiment-in-the-Wild)). In simulation on DROID variants, MolmoAct2-DROID averages **37.7%**, and only **26.7%** on pick-and-place ([MolmoAct2](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.02881)). A policy that manages a quarter of pick-and-place trials on its home embodiment will do less on SP7.

## Wrist view, state representation and frames decide transfer

Two controlled 2026 studies identify the levers that matter most. They are the same levers an SP7 adapter controls. Cloak attributes the dominant cross-embodiment failure to the policy seeing an unfamiliar end effector in the wrist camera. Masking it raised the YAM arm from 43.8% to 70.8% ([Cloak](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.22836v1)). ZETA found that switching from absolute EEF state to **EEF-delta** state raised arm-only transfer from about 39% to about 74% in simulation. It also deliberately aligned cameras so the gripper's image-plane distribution matched training ([ZETA](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.02546v1)). Penn's tests show how fragile the wrist channel is: blocking π0-FAST-DROID's wrist camera froze the robot at 0% success ([Penn PAL Lab](https://penn-pal-lab.github.io/Pi0-Experiment-in-the-Wild)).

The low-level conventions are equally unforgiving. OXE delta-EE models must be un-normalized with a dataset key. MolmoAct2's per-tag statistics show Bridge translation q99 near 0.03, against a Fractal standard deviation near 0.06–0.07 ([norm_stats.json](https://huggingface.co/allenai/MolmoAct2/blob/main/norm_stats.json)), so a wrong key rescales motion several-fold. The SP7 adapter therefore has to make each of these choices explicitly per checkpoint:

- **Normalization key**, for models that un-normalize per dataset.
- **Delta frame**: base-frame or EE-local.
- **Euler order**, for Euler-based outputs.
- **Translation units**: LAP uses centimetres ([LAP](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.10556v1)).
- **Gripper polarity**: no primary file reviewed states whether 1 means open or closed.

These choices then need to be checked empirically in simulation. In practice, SP7's two-finger prismatic gripper is the easy part. Cloak maps the model's gripper scalar straight to jaw opening rather than through IK, and ZETA's gripper-only shift held 78% ([ZETA](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.02546v1)). For SP7, a [0, 1] → metres stroke mapping should be low-risk.

## A modular grasp pipeline is the honest zero-shot answer

If "zero-shot" means no SP7 data of any kind, the strongest released components are not VLAs. **GraspGen-X** is a single cross-embodiment 6-DoF diffusion grasp model conditioned on a gripper's swept volume. It is trained on more than 2 billion grasps across 32 procedural grippers, and it targets unseen grippers from a URDF plus a wizard-generated config with a `parallel_2f` type. Code is Apache-2.0 and weights use the NVIDIA Open Model License ([GraspGenX](https://github.com/NVlabs/GraspGenX)). It runs in about 3 ms for 150 candidates under TensorRT and exports USD for Isaac Sim. NVIDIA says it removes per-gripper training ([NVIDIA blog](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/cvpr-research-grasping-driving-agent-training/)). Robot use needs instance segmentation and collision filtering against a scene point cloud ([GraspGen](https://github.com/NVlabs/GraspGen)). In Isaac Lab, simulated depth and segmentation make that trivial. On the real SP7, a calibrated depth camera becomes effectively mandatory. No success rate for unseen parallel grippers was found, and Newton compatibility of its USD export is untested.

The place step can be rule-based, since box and stack targets are geometrically simple. It can also come from a training-free VLM planner. VoxPoser is MIT-licensed. ReKep needs GPT-4o and ships no real-world perception stack ([ReKep](https://github.com/huangwl18/ReKep)). A 2026 training-free system that routes objectives through a Molmo2 model to predefined pick-and-place primitives reports **62% end-to-end success** ([alphaXiv 2606.23157](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.23157)). That suggests the locally served Molmo could fill the planner role without an API dependency. The outputs of this whole pipeline are EE waypoints, which is exactly what the task-folder `expert.py` and arm-declared `ik` constraints already execute. For cube tasks, the remaining failure modes are perception and calibration, which are measurable and fixable. They are not opaque embodiment-gap failures.

Other "zero-shot cross-embodiment" methods do not fit SP7's no-data constraint as well as they first appear. KITE, Cloak and SkelWAM need no target-robot data, but each needs a policy already trained on a source robot for the task. KITE's released assets are a MuJoCo placing task, and its repo has no licence file ([KITE](https://github.com/helloqxwang/KITE)). DreamZero adapts to a new arm with about **30 minutes of play data** and fine-tuning on at least two H100/GB200-class GPUs ([DreamZero](https://github.com/dreamzero0/dreamzero)). That is a few-shot method, not a zero-shot one.

## Recommended path: one zero-shot baseline, one cheap probe, one real fix

Start with the modular pipeline in Isaac Lab 3.0, because it is the only option likely to reach high success on cube-in-box and stacking without SP7 data. It also produces confirmed successful episodes through the shared `sim.datagen.rollout` path.

In parallel, run a bounded zero-shot probe of GR00T N1.7 because the serving infrastructure already exists. The probe needs a new policy-folder adapter that keeps model conventions inside the folder, per the `configure(ArmInfo)` contract. The adapter has to do four things:

1. Feed SP7's FK pose as `eef_9d` in a DROID-like base frame.
2. Convert the relative xyz + 6D action through a rotation matrix to an XYZW quaternion for the IK.
3. Map the absolute gripper value to prismatic stroke.
4. Clip per-step deltas to the tag's statistics.

LAP-3B is the second probe target. It has the best published unseen-arm claim and an Apache-2.0 licence, but it needs a new client, and its delta EE is expressed in centimetres and Euler angles. Optionally add the Cloak-style wrist mask, which is the single largest documented gain, before drawing conclusions. Expect single-digit to roughly 40% success. Treat a sim result without visual matching as a weak predictor of real performance, since SimplerEnv's sim–real correlation depended on visual matching ([SimplerEnv](https://github.com/simpler-env/SimplerEnv)).

The fix most likely to succeed is the one the project is already equipped for. Generate 50–100 scripted or Mimic episodes per task on SP7 in simulation. Then post-train GR00T N1.7 with a `NEW_EMBODIMENT` modality config, which NVIDIA requires for unsupported robots ([policy.md](https://github.com/NVIDIA/Isaac-GR00T/blob/main/getting_started/policy.md)), and add a small real set later to close the visual gap. Published adaptations sit in this range: GR00T N1.5 reached **47.5% on RoboCasa with 30 demos per task**, and ZETA used 50 real demos per task ([GR00T N1.5](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/gear/gr00t-n1_5/), [ZETA](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.02546v1)). Fine-tuning needs a larger GPU than inference. One guide puts GR00T N1.6 fine-tuning above 48 GB ([Seeed](https://wiki.seeedstudio.com/fine_tune_gr00t_n1.6_for_lerobot_so_arm_and_deploy_on_agx_orin/)). On the real Feetech arm, smooth and rate-limit commanded chunks, and validate every adapter in simulation with action limits first, as the MolmoAct2 card itself advises.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the question. What makes a checkpoint usable on SP7 is not its size or benchmark score but what the adapter presents to it. Successful cross-embodiment transfer in 2026 comes from making a new robot look like a known one: relative EEF actions, retargeted kinematics, a masked wrist view and aligned cameras. SP7's seven revolute joints and parallel jaw make a Franka/DROID disguise unusually feasible. Retargeting effort therefore pays off more than trying additional checkpoints.

The license, frame-convention and visual-gap uncertainties all argue for treating learned zero-shot as an experiment, not a deliverable. GR00T N1.7's gated backbone and MolmoAct2's missing licence tag also need checking before any commercial use. The "no demonstrations" constraint is better met by the project's own scripted simulation data generator than by any foundation model. Scripted sim demonstrations satisfy the no-leader-arm requirement while giving the policy what every published success relied on: data from the robot it is controlling.
